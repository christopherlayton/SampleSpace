# Shape of the Data

Centre and spread don't tell you the **shape**. Two numbers do: skewness and kurtosis.

## Skewness

- **0**: symmetric, like a normal curve.
- **Positive** (right) skew: a long tail of high values. The mean sits *above* the median. Income, reaction times and mission lengths look like this.
- **Negative** (left) skew: a long tail of low values. The mean sits *below* the median. Scores on an easy test look like this.

## Kurtosis

Reported as *excess* kurtosis, so a normal curve scores **0**. Positive values mean heavier tails (more extreme values than a normal curve would give); negative values mean lighter tails.

## What to do about it

Shape guides what you report and which tests suit the data:

- For skewed variables, report the **median and IQR** alongside (or instead of) the mean and SD.
- Look for outliers and find out *why* they're there before deciding anything. In the Crew Log the long missions aren't errors; they're space stations.
- Many tests assume normally distributed residuals or sampling distributions, not raw data. You'll meet those assumptions in the Means Cluster.

> **From the Crew Log:** mission length has a mean of 1,051 hours, a median of 261 hours, and a skew of about 2.1. Hold that thought for Plot Nebula, where you'll *see* the tail.
